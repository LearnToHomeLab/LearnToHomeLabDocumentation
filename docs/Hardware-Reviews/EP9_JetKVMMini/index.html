JetKVM Mini Review – A $39 KVM Over IP for Your Homelab!

If you're running a homelab, managing multiple servers, or just want an affordable way to remotely access your computers, the JetKVM Mini might be one of the most interesting budget KVM over IP devices available!

In this video, we're taking a look at the JetKVM Mini, a compact KVM over IP device that costs just $39! We'll unbox it, go over its hardware and specifications, explore the software interface, and compare it to some of the other popular KVM over IP solutions on the market.

We'll also discuss one of the biggest potential drawbacks I found with the JetKVM Mini: mounting it inside a 10-inch server rack.

What Is the JetKVM Mini?​

The JetKVM Mini is a small KVM over IP device that allows you to remotely access and control a computer or server without needing to be physically in front of it.

Unlike traditional remote desktop software, a KVM over IP device gives you access to the computer's video output and allows you to interact with it using a keyboard and mouse, even when the operating system isn't running.

This makes it useful for:

  • Remotely accessing your homelab servers.
  • Troubleshooting computers that won't boot.
  • Accessing BIOS and UEFI settings.
  • Installing or reinstalling operating systems.
  • Managing headless computers without a dedicated monitor, keyboard, or mouse.

For anyone building a homelab, this can be an extremely useful tool!

Unboxing and Hardware Overview​

We start the video by unboxing the JetKVM Mini and taking a closer look at its physical design, available connections, and overall build quality.

One of the first things you'll notice is just how small this device is. Compared to many traditional KVM over IP solutions, the JetKVM Mini takes up very little space.

We also take a closer look at its hardware specifications, port layout, and built-in display.

While its compact size is certainly a benefit, the positioning of its ports creates some interesting challenges when it comes to mounting the device in a server rack, which we'll discuss later.

Software Overview and Remote Management​

Next, we explore the JetKVM Mini's software interface and the remote management capabilities it offers.

One of the biggest advantages of using a dedicated KVM over IP device is that you don't need to rely on the operating system of the computer you're managing.

Whether you're troubleshooting a failed boot, changing BIOS settings, or installing a new operating system, you can still interact with the connected computer remotely.

In the video, we walk through the software and take a look at how the JetKVM Mini works from a management perspective.

JetKVM Mini vs. GL.iNet, USBridge KVM, and PiKVM​

One of the biggest selling points of the JetKVM Mini is its $39 price tag.

There are several other KVM over IP solutions available, including devices from GL.iNet, USBridge, and PiKVM.

While these alternatives offer their own hardware designs, features, and capabilities, the JetKVM Mini enters the market at a very attractive price point.

In the video, we compare the cost of these different solutions and discuss what makes the JetKVM Mini an interesting option for someone building a budget homelab.

Of course, price isn't everything. Hardware capabilities, connectivity, software features, and how easily a device fits into your setup are all important considerations.

The Problem With Mounting the JetKVM Mini in a 10-Inch Server Rack​

This is one of the biggest design considerations I noticed with the JetKVM Mini, especially for anyone using a 10-inch server rack.

Although the device itself is extremely compact, its port placement can make rack mounting more complicated than you might initially expect.

The main issue is that the ports are positioned along the top of the device when its display is facing outward.

If you want to mount the JetKVM Mini vertically with its screen visible from the front of your rack, you'll need additional clearance above the device for the HDMI and USB-C cables.

With standard cables connected, the total height can exceed 100 mm, making it difficult to fit into a traditional rack-mounted configuration.

Even when using 90-degree HDMI and USB-C cables, the overall height would still exceed a standard 1U rack space (44.45 mm).

A Possible Solution: A 45-Degree Rack Mount​

One potential solution would be to design or 3D print a custom mount that positions the JetKVM Mini at approximately a 45-degree angle.

This would allow the device to sit at an angle inside the rack, with the cables routed toward the rear rather than extending directly upward.

The display would also be angled slightly downward, allowing you to look down at the screen from the front of the rack.

For anyone running a compact 10-inch rack, this could be a much cleaner mounting solution.

However, it's still something that would need to be designed around the available rack space, cable clearance, and the dimensions of the device.

I would have liked to see a port arrangement that made front-facing rack mounting easier, especially considering how useful a device like this could be in a homelab environment.

Final Thoughts – Is the JetKVM Mini Worth $39?​

At just $39, the JetKVM Mini is an interesting option for anyone looking to add remote management capabilities to their homelab without spending a lot of money.

Its small size, KVM over IP functionality, and software interface make it worth considering alongside other solutions like GL.iNet, USBridge KVM, and PiKVM.

My biggest concern is the physical port layout, particularly for users who want to mount the device neatly inside a compact 10-inch server rack.

While this isn't necessarily a dealbreaker, it's something worth considering before purchasing, especially if you're planning to integrate it into a custom rack-mounted setup.

Overall, the JetKVM Mini offers an affordable way to get started with KVM over IP, and I'm interested to see what mounting solutions the homelab community comes up with for this device!
