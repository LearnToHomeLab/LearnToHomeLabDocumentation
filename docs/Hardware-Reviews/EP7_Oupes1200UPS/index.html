Oupes 1200 UPS Review (battery Backup)

If you're running a homelab, a UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) is one of those pieces of equipment that's easy to overlook—until the power goes out.

Whether you're running a NAS, a Proxmox server, a network switch, a firewall, or an entire rack of equipment, unexpected power loss can cause interrupted services, corrupted data, and unnecessary headaches. A UPS helps protect your equipment by providing battery backup when utility power fails.

In this video, I take a look at the OUPES UPS 1200, a compact lithium-based UPS designed to provide backup power for home labs, servers, networking equipment, and other electronics.

What Is the OUPES UPS 1200?​

The OUPES UPS 1200 is a battery-backed power protection device featuring a 368Wh LiFePO₄ battery and a rated output of up to 1,200W. Unlike many traditional UPS units that rely on relatively small lead-acid batteries, the UPS 1200 uses lithium iron phosphate battery technology.

That makes it an interesting option for homelab users who want a compact UPS with a higher power output and a battery designed for a longer service life.

Some of its advertised specifications include:

  • Battery capacity: 368Wh
  • Rated output: 1,200W
  • Battery chemistry: LiFePO₄ (lithium iron phosphate)
  • AC outlets: 8
  • DC outputs: 2
  • Power protection: Online double-conversion mode with advertised 0ms transfer
  • Operating modes: Online and Eco
  • Monitoring: Mobile app and computer monitoring support

You can find the full product details on the official OUPES UPS 1200 product page.

Comparing the OUPES UPS 1200 to Traditional UPS Models​

I've also been comparing the OUPES UPS 1200 against a couple of more traditional UPS units: the CyberPower LX1500GU3 and the APC Back-UPS 650VA.

These are all designed to provide backup power, but they take different approaches to battery capacity, power output, and overall design.

The CyberPower LX1500GU3 has more total outlets, but only five of them provide battery backup. The APC Back-UPS 650VA is a smaller, more traditional UPS designed primarily for basic computer and networking backup.

For my homelab, I find the OUPES UPS 1200 to be the more interesting option.

The larger battery capacity is a major advantage. With 368Wh of storage, it has considerably more energy available than either of the traditional UPS models in this comparison. That means the potential for longer runtimes with the same connected load.

I also like that all eight AC outlets on the OUPES are intended to provide backup power. With traditional UPS units, you often have to be careful about which outlets you use because some are only surge-protected.

For homelab equipment, having more battery-backed outlets available makes it easier to connect the equipment I actually want to keep running.

Battery Capacity and Runtime​

One of the biggest differences between these UPS units is the amount of energy they can store.

A UPS rated for 1,200W does not necessarily run equipment for a long time. The wattage rating describes how much power it can deliver, while the watt-hour rating describes how much energy is stored in its battery.

For example, a 100W load theoretically consumes 100Wh every hour.

With the OUPES UPS 1200's 368Wh battery, that gives it a theoretical runtime of around 3.68 hours at 100W before accounting for inverter losses and other overhead.

Actual runtime will be lower, but it still provides substantially more battery capacity than the smaller traditional UPS models.

This is important for a homelab. If I'm only trying to keep my NAS, networking equipment, and a few low-power servers running during a short outage, I want as much usable battery capacity as I can get.

The Important Issue: The Outlets Stay On Until the Battery Is Depleted​

There is one important behavior of the OUPES UPS 1200 that I discuss in the video.

The output ports remain powered during an outage until the battery is depleted.

This is something to be aware of when using the UPS with homelab equipment.

Traditional UPS units often work alongside shutdown software that can monitor the battery status and initiate a graceful shutdown of a computer or server before the battery is exhausted. This is especially important for NAS systems and virtualization hosts.

With the OUPES, you need to consider how your equipment will respond to an extended outage. If your connected devices continue running and the battery eventually runs out, they can lose power abruptly.

For a homelab, this means you should not assume that the UPS will automatically shut down your equipment at the appropriate time. You need to make sure your monitoring and shutdown strategy is compatible with the UPS.

This is particularly relevant if you're running TrueNAS, Proxmox, or other systems where an orderly shutdown is important.

The additional battery capacity is great, but it is still important to plan for what happens when that battery is finally exhausted.

Why I Prefer the OUPES UPS 1200​

For my homelab, I find the OUPES UPS 1200 to be a better option than the traditional UPS models I've compared it against.

The biggest reasons are:

  • More battery capacity: The 368Wh battery provides substantially more stored energy than the smaller lead-acid units.
  • Higher output power: The 1,200W rating provides more headroom for connected equipment.
  • Lithium battery technology: LiFePO₄ batteries offer advantages in cycle life compared with traditional lead-acid batteries.
  • All eight AC outlets provide backup power: This is convenient when connecting multiple pieces of homelab equipment.
  • Online double-conversion mode: OUPES advertises a 0ms transfer time in this mode.
  • A more flexible backup power solution: Its capacity and output make it an interesting option for servers, NAS systems, and networking equipment.

The CyberPower and APC units are still useful UPS options, especially for smaller setups or users who only need enough backup time to safely shut down their computers.

However, when I look at the battery capacity and the features offered by the OUPES UPS 1200, I find it to be a more compelling option for my homelab.

The main thing to keep in mind is the behavior of the output ports during an extended outage. You need to plan for your equipment to shut down safely before the battery is completely exhausted.

Final Thoughts​

A UPS is an important part of building a reliable homelab. It helps protect your equipment from unexpected power loss and gives you time to keep critical services running or shut everything down safely.

The OUPES UPS 1200 is an interesting alternative to traditional desktop UPS units. Its combination of a 1,200W output rating, 368Wh lithium battery, multiple battery-backed outlets, and online double-conversion design makes it worth considering for home servers and networking setups.

For my use case, I prefer the OUPES over the CyberPower LX1500GU3 and APC Back-UPS 650VA because of its larger battery capacity and the flexibility it provides.

Just remember that a larger battery does not automatically mean your equipment will shut down safely. You still need to plan for how your homelab will respond when the power goes out and the battery eventually runs out.

Check out the video above to learn more details on what I mean, when I say that the device runs into a fundamental flaw on not turning off the AC ports unless the battery also completely dies.
